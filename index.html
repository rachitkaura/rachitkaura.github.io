Case study — Multi-agent personal AI system

A Chief-of-Staff orchestrator with specialized sub-agents

STACK: CLAUDE CODE · MARKDOWN SPECS · STRUCTURED HAND-OFFS

Problem

One generalist assistant does everything adequately and nothing consistently. I wanted domain expertise that persists: a trainer that knows my movement constraints, a nutrition coach that knows my macros and pantry, a money coach that knows my accounts — coordinated, not siloed.

Architecture

A Chief-of-Staff orchestrator receives every request, decides which sub-agent owns it, and manages hand-offs when a task spans domains — a training plan that changes calorie targets touches two agents. Each agent is defined by a spec: scope, tone, tools, and what it must escalate rather than decide.

Guardrails

  • Every agent spec declares explicit out-of-scope actions and escalation paths.
  • Cross-domain changes route through the orchestrator — no agent silently rewrites another's outputs.
  • The same human-in-the-loop pattern regulated enterprises need: authority is designed, not assumed.

What broke, and what it taught me

  • Vague scope boundaries caused agents to answer outside their lane — fixed by rewriting specs with explicit refusal criteria.
  • Hand-offs lost context until I standardized a structured brief each agent passes forward.
  • Lesson: agent systems fail at the seams. Design the seams first.

How I work

PRINCIPLE 01

Design the seams first

Systems — and teams — fail at hand-off points, not inside well-run lanes. I spec interfaces, ownership, and escalation before anything else, whether it's two agents or twenty SMEs.

PRINCIPLE 02

Automate what repeats

A 60-minute manual cycle became 8 minutes because I scripted it myself. If a task happens weekly, it's a candidate for automation — and the person closest to it should build the first version.

PRINCIPLE 03

Authority is designed, not assumed

Years in government delivery taught me why guardrails exist. The AI systems that make it into production in banks and agencies are the ones where a human holds the pen at the right moments.
